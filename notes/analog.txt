What an ADC actually does (30 second version)
Your Arduino's analog pins don't read "voltage" directly as a number; they read a voltage between 0V and 5V (or 3.3V depending on board) and convert it to a number between 0 and 1023 (that's the ADC — analog-to-digital converter). So when your EMG sensor outputs "muscle is flexing," what you're actually seeing on the Arduino side is just a number bouncing around in that 0-1023 range. Resting muscle might sit around, say, 300-400 (totally sensor-dependent, you'll see your actual baseline once it's wired up), and a hard flex might spike it toward 600-800. There's no universal "this number means flexed"; you have to observe your own sensor's behavior first.
